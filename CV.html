Research profile

I study how neural circuits transform noisy, distributed sensory information into a single committed action. My postdoctoral work established two results. First, visual approach-detecting neurons integrate cues in the natural temporal order those cues occur during a real approach event — a previously unrecognised strategy I termed sequenced cue integration. Second, as co-lead of a collaborative project, I discovered the first neural circuit in any animal that computes the direction of motion of an odour signal.

I am extending these toward multisensory integration, behavioural arbitration under conflicting drives, and bilateral sensory comparison, using Drosophila as a system where these questions are quantitatively tractable. I combine quantitative behaviour, two-photon imaging, optogenetics, genetic perturbation and modelling, with a background in experimental and theoretical physics.

Technical expertise

Python and MATLAB; quantitative behavioural analysis; time-series and multivariate statistics; dynamical-systems and phenomenological modelling.
