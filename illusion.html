Try the illusion

A demonstration of the luminance-induced expansion aftereffect — Lexa — from Vashistha & Clark.

How to view it. Sit a comfortable distance from the screen, press play, and fix your gaze on the centre of the pattern without moving your eyes. Keep looking for the full clip. When it ends, look at a blank grey or white area of the screen.

The blank area should appear to expand outwards for a second or two. That percept is the aftereffect: adapting to a purely temporal luminance change — a decreasing sawtooth ramp, with no spatial displacement anywhere in the stimulus — is enough to produce an illusion of expansion. No spatial gradient is needed.

{% include video.html src="lexa-ramp" caption="A uniform field on a repeating decreasing luminance ramp. Fixate the centre for the whole clip, then look at a blank part of the screen." %}

If you see nothing the first time, try again in a dim room and hold fixation more steadily — the effect depends on adaptation, so eye movements weaken it.
