Harsh Vashistha

Postdoctoral Associate, Clark Lab · Yale University

I study how neural circuits turn noisy, distributed sensory information into a single committed action.

I recently discovered that visual approach-detecting neurons integrate cues in the natural temporal order those cues occur during a real approach — a strategy I call sequenced cue integration. And in collaboration with Thierry Emonet's group, we found the first neural circuit in any animal that computes the direction of motion of an odour signal, independent of wind.

I came to neuroscience from physics. My PhD, with Hanna Salman at the University of Pittsburgh, used custom microfluidics to track single E. coli across many generations and ask how physical traits like cell size are inherited and controlled.

Research Publications CV Email

On the faculty job market for Fall 2027.

My independent lab will ask how context determines what a sensory computation is used for, and which drive wins when several compete. I work in Drosophila, where the circuits are small enough to manipulate cell by cell. The lab will combine closed-loop behaviour, optogenetics, two-photon imaging and quantitative modelling to connect circuit-level computation to behaviour, and to find where in the brain that decision is made.
