Harsh Vashistha

Postdoctoral Associate, Clark Lab · Yale University

I study how small brains turn messy sensory input into decisions — how a fly works out that something is moving, where it is coming from, and whether it matters.

My work sits at the intersection of neuroscience, biophysics and sensory ecology. Using Drosophila melanogaster, I pair psychophysical behavioural experiments with live two-photon calcium imaging to dissect the circuit-level computations that underlie perception — in vision, and more recently in olfaction, where we found that flies compute the direction of odour motion itself rather than inferring it from wind.

I came to neuroscience from physics. My PhD, with Hanna Salman at the University of Pittsburgh, used custom microfluidics to track single E. coli across many generations and ask how physical traits like cell size are inherited and controlled. That training is still how I approach circuits: build the measurement, then build the model.
