Harsh Vashistha

Postdoctoral Associate, Clark Lab · Yale University

I study how neural circuits turn noisy, distributed sensory information into a single committed action.

Two results from my postdoc frame the work. Visual approach-detecting neurons turn out to integrate cues in the natural temporal order those cues occur during a real approach — a strategy I call sequenced cue integration. And, co-leading a collaboration with Thierry Emonet's group, we found the first neural circuit in any animal that computes the direction of motion of an odour signal, independent of wind.

I'm extending both toward multisensory integration, behavioural arbitration under conflicting drives, and bilateral sensory comparison — using Drosophila melanogaster, where these questions stay quantitatively tractable. The toolkit is quantitative behaviour, two-photon imaging, optogenetics, genetic perturbation and modelling.

I came to neuroscience from physics. My PhD, with Hanna Salman at the University of Pittsburgh, used custom microfluidics to track single E. coli across many generations and ask how physical traits like cell size are inherited and controlled. That training is still how I approach circuits: build the measurement, then build the model.
